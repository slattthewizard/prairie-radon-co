# Manitoba Hydro Radon Loan: Terms, Limits and How It Works

Most people who find a high radon result in Manitoba hit the same second question within a day: how do I pay for this? There's a loan that was built for part of that answer, and hardly anyone uses it.

Manitoba Hydro's Home Energy Efficiency Loan covers radon mitigation. This post sets out the terms as Manitoba Hydro publishes them, who it suits, and how it can sit alongside the Lungs Matter grant.

## What the Manitoba Hydro radon loan offers

These are the terms on Manitoba Hydro's loan page when we last read it:

| Term | What Manitoba Hydro says |
|---|---|
| Radon mitigation loan limit | $5,000 |
| Repayment term | 5 years |
| Interest rate | 6.2% (O.A.C.) for the first five years |
| Down payment | None |
| Overall cap | $12,500 per residence |

"O.A.C." means on approved credit, so the rate isn't a promise until Manitoba Hydro has approved you. Payments go on your energy bill rather than a separate loan statement. The $5,000 radon amount counts toward the $12,500 limit per residence, which matters if you're also planning insulation, windows or a heat pump under the same loan.

A February 2026 jurisdictional scan tabled in the Northwest Territories Legislative Assembly adds one more point: "There are no income thresholds for eligibility." That's useful, because most radon help in the Prairies is aimed at low-income households. For terms that can change, read the source: [Manitoba Hydro, Home Energy Efficiency Loan](https://www.hydro.mb.ca/account/loans/home-energy-efficiency-loan/).

## The one condition that matters: a C-NRPP contractor

Manitoba Hydro says: "To be eligible for financing, radon mitigation must be performed through a C-NRPP certified contractor."

That has a practical effect on how you shop. A handyman who offers to "do the radon thing" for less isn't eligible, however good the work. Before you sign anything, check the company's certification on the [C-NRPP find-a-professional tool](https://c-nrpp.ca/find-a-professional/). Our post on [what C-NRPP certification means](/blog/what-is-c-nrpp-certification/) explains what the certification covers.

Certification is voluntary in both provinces. Health Canada recommends it, and money is one place where it becomes a requirement.

## Will $5,000 cover a mitigation system?

Possibly, but nobody can promise it. Here's what is published for the Prairies. Winnipeg installers advertise systems from about $1,500 to $3,000, with one adding 20% for installs between 30 November and 20 May. A Regina installer quotes $2,500 to $3,500, and says systems with a crawl space or several suction points could be much higher. Those are each contractor's own advertised figures, not a market survey, and none of them is a quote for your house.

Health Canada's 2014 reduction guide put the national cost at $2,000 to $3,000, and Take Action on Radon's 2022 figure for an average installation was approximately $3,000. Both are national, not Manitoba prices. Our post on [radon mitigation cost in Manitoba and Saskatchewan](/blog/radon-mitigation-cost-manitoba-saskatchewan/) lays out who published what.

Set against a $5,000 limit, the advertised Winnipeg range sits comfortably under it. A complicated house might not. Get a written quote first, then compare it with the loan limit, not the other way round. Our [radon mitigation quote checklist](/blog/radon-mitigation-quote-checklist/) lists what a quote should spell out.

Interest and monthly payment figures depend on your approved amount, and Manitoba Hydro will give you its own numbers. We haven't worked any out here, because they'd be our arithmetic, not theirs.

## The loan is barely used

This is the odd part. The Made-in-Manitoba Radon Action Plan says Manitoba Hydro financed "only an average of seven mitigation systems" per year over the last decade. It's a small number for a province where Health Canada's 2012 survey found 23.7% of tested homes above the guideline.

The same plan reports a public attitude figure: only 27% of Manitoba and Saskatchewan survey respondents thought $2,000 to $3,000 was a reasonable price to reduce radon levels. Cost is clearly a barrier. Lungs Matter has a similar story: the plan says only 18 Manitoba households used the funds in 2024.

We can't tell you why uptake is low. What we can say is that if you're weighing a quote, the loan is worth a phone call.

## Pairing the loan with Lungs Matter

Manitoba's other support is Lungs Matter, run by the Manitoba Lung Association. It pays "up to $1,500 towards the cost of radon mitigation when completed by a radon mitigation professional." The Lung Association describes it as the only radon mitigation grant programme offered in Manitoba.

It's aimed at Manitoba residents diagnosed with lung cancer, or in a "low to moderate-income household", with priority to low-income households. The rules that trip people up:

- You need a long-term 90-day C-NRPP approved test kit result first. Homeowner-level digital monitors don't qualify.
- The work has to be done by a radon mitigation professional.
- Applications are assessed monthly.

The Canadian Lung Association also runs a national Lungs Matter program, open to Manitobans, with up to $1,500 for households whose income is below the provincial median. That version needs a long-term C-NRPP test result above 200 Bq/m³ and a quote from a C-NRPP certified professional, won't pay for mitigation already completed, and says funding is limited. Either way, apply before you book the install.

On paper the two fit together: the grant lowers what you need to borrow, and the loan covers what's left. We haven't found anything saying whether Manitoba Hydro and Lungs Matter can be used on the same job, or how the approval timing works between them. Ask both before you book anything.

If your household isn't low or moderate income and nobody has a lung cancer diagnosis, Lungs Matter probably isn't an option, and the loan is the more relevant one, since it has no income threshold. ## A sensible order of steps

1. **Test first.** A long-term test of at least 91 days, in the lowest lived-in level. If you plan to apply for Lungs Matter, use a long-term, 90-day C-NRPP approved kit.
2. **Check the number.** Health Canada says to mitigate within 1 year if you're above 200 Bq/m³, and sooner the higher the reading.
3. **Get a written quote** from a C-NRPP certified mitigation professional.
4. **Apply before you book.** Ask Manitoba Hydro about the loan and the Lung Association about the grant, and don't start work until you know what each will approve.
5. **Retest after the install.** Health Canada recommends a short-term test by a certified professional once the system is on, a long-term test in the first heating season, and a retest every five years.

For how the system itself works, see [how a radon mitigation system works](/blog/how-a-radon-mitigation-system-works/). Health Canada says that in most homes mitigation reduces the radon level by more than 80%.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much can I borrow from Manitoba Hydro for radon mitigation?

The radon mitigation loan limit is $5,000, repaid over 5 years at 6.2% (O.A.C.) for the first five years, with no down payment. It counts toward the $12,500 limit per residence on the Home Energy Efficiency Loan.

### Does the Manitoba Hydro loan have an income test?

No income threshold is listed. A February 2026 scan of public radon supports, tabled in the Northwest Territories Legislative Assembly, says there are no income thresholds for eligibility. Approval still depends on credit, since the rate is quoted as O.A.C.

### Does the contractor need to be certified?

Yes. Manitoba Hydro says radon mitigation must be performed through a C-NRPP certified contractor to be eligible for financing. You can check a company's certification using the C-NRPP find-a-professional tool before you sign a quote.

### Can I use the loan and the Lungs Matter grant together?

We haven't found anything that says they can or can't be combined. Lungs Matter pays up to $1,500 for qualifying households, and the national version won't cover work already done, so ask both Manitoba Hydro and the Manitoba Lung Association before you book an install.
