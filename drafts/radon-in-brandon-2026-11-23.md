# Radon in Brandon, Manitoba: What the Survey Numbers Say and What to Do

Brandon sits near the top of the radon tables, and it has for a while. In Health Canada's 2012 Cross-Canada survey, the Brandon Regional Health Authority posted the highest share of homes above the guideline of any health region in Manitoba or Saskatchewan. The 2024 survey also names Brandon among Canadian cities where a quarter to a half of tested homes are at or above 200 Bq/m³.

None of that says anything about your house. It does say that a Brandon homeowner who has never tested is guessing, and the odds aren't in the guess's favour.

## What the 2012 Health Canada survey found in Brandon

The 2012 survey used long-term tests of at least three months, placed in the lowest lived-in level of each home. Here is how the Brandon area compared with the rest of Manitoba.

| Area (2012 health region) | Homes tested | % above 200 Bq/m³ | % above 600 Bq/m³ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Brandon Regional Health Authority | 79 | 44.3 | 3.8 |
| Assiniboine Regional Health Authority (region around Brandon) | 110 | 34.5 | 1.8 |
| Winnipeg Regional Health Authority | 66 | 12.1 | 0.0 |
| Manitoba total | 1,479 | 23.7 | 2.6 |

Two cautions. These are health regions, not city limits, and the 2012 boundaries apply. And 79 homes is a small sample, so treat 44.3% as a signal, not a precise figure. Still, the Assiniboine region next door also came in well above the provincial total. Western Manitoba as a whole looks like a place where radon is common.

## What the 2024 survey adds

The 2024 Cross-Canada Survey on Radon, run by the Evict Radon National Study with Health Canada, CAREX Canada and the BC Centre for Disease Control, pooled long-term test results from 2009 to 2024. For Brandon it lists:

- 36.1% of properties at or above 200 Bq/m³
- a geometric mean of 147.0 Bq/m³
- a maximum observed reading of 559 Bq/m³
- data from only about 50 to 99 properties

The report says these city figures are unweighted: there wasn't enough information to apply weighting, and the values "may increase or decrease somewhat once appropriately weighted." They come from a volunteer sample, not a random one.

Please don't read the 2012 and 2024 numbers as a trend. The samples and methods differ, and a drop from 44.3% to 36.1% on paper doesn't mean Brandon got better. What the two surveys agree on is the broad picture: a lot of Brandon-area homes test above 200.

The Made-in-Manitoba Radon Action Plan, released in November 2025, says the 2024 survey found 43% of Manitoba homes over the guideline. We're attributing that to the plan, because the survey report itself says roughly 1 in 3 for Saskatchewan and Manitoba on an unweighted basis. Either way, it's a lot.

## What 200 Bq/m³ means for a Brandon home

The Canadian guideline is 200 becquerels per cubic metre, measured as the average annual level in the normal occupancy area of the home: anywhere you spend more than 4 hours a day. A finished basement or a basement suite counts. A furnace room or an unfinished storage corner doesn't.

Health Canada says the risk below the guideline is small but no level is risk-free, and that every home should be tested, wherever it is and whatever it's built like. If a long-term test comes back above 200, Health Canada recommends taking action within 1 year, sooner for higher readings. No Manitoba law requires a homeowner to test or fix anything. That choice is yours.

## Testing in Brandon: library monitors and long-term kits

There are two practical routes.

**Borrow a screening monitor.** The Western Manitoba Regional Library, with branches in Brandon, Carberry, Glenboro, Hartney and Neepawa, is a partner in the Manitoba Lung Association's library lending program. We couldn't find published loan terms for the Brandon library, so call the branch about loan periods and availability. Health Canada's guidance, repeated by Take Action on Radon, is that lending programs run from October to March, during the heating season.

A borrowed monitor is a screening tool. It can tell you whether a proper test is worth doing soon, but Health Canada says short-term measurements aren't appropriate for deciding on mitigation.

**Buy a long-term kit.** HomeRadonTest.ca, the ordering site used by both provincial lung associations, charges $70.00 per 90-day kit before tax, with lab analysis at the Saskatchewan Research Council included. The Manitoba government puts retail kits at generally $50 to $100. Health Canada wants at least 91 days of the test inside the heating season, so the Manitoba government's October-to-April window suits Brandon well.

Put the kit in the lowest level you actually live in, in the breathing zone, at least 30 cm above the floor and 30 cm from walls and ceilings. Keep it clear of sump holes, vents, windows and exposed foundation cracks. ## Fixing a high reading: what we can and can't say about price

A sub-slab depressurization system is the usual fix: a pipe through the floor slab, a fan that runs continuously, and a vent that carries soil gas outside. Health Canada says active systems in most homes reduce the level by more than 80%, and its 2026 factsheet says 90% or more. Installation can generally be done in a day.

Now the part Brandon readers usually ask about first. We couldn't find a published mitigation price for Brandon. No local contractor page we read lists one, so we aren't going to guess. ## Money help for Manitoba homeowners

Brandon is in Manitoba, so two Manitoba programs apply.

**Manitoba Hydro Home Energy Efficiency Loan.** The radon mitigation limit is $5,000 over 5 years, at 6.2% (O.A.C.) for the first five years. Payments go on your energy bill with no down payment, and the loan counts toward a $12,500 limit per residence. The contractor must be C-NRPP certified. There are no income thresholds. Uptake has been low: the Made-in-Manitoba plan says Hydro financed only an average of seven mitigation systems a year over the last decade.

**Lungs Matter.** The Manitoba Lung Association offers a payment of up to $1,500 toward mitigation done by a radon mitigation professional. It's for Manitobans diagnosed with lung cancer or in a low to moderate income household, with priority to low-income households. It needs a long-term 90-day C-NRPP approved test kit result, and homeowner-level digital monitors don't qualify. Applications are assessed monthly. Our [Manitoba grants post](/blog/radon-mitigation-grants-manitoba/) covers the rules.

## New builds in Brandon

Manitoba adopted the 2010 National Building Code, with its radon rough-in, as the Manitoba Building Code on 1 April 2011. A rough-in is a capped pipe under the slab. As the City of Regina's advisory puts it, the rough-in "does not actively reduce radon levels" until a fan is added. A newer Brandon home still needs a long-term test. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is radon really higher in Brandon than in other Manitoba cities?

In the 2012 Health Canada survey, the Brandon health region had 44.3% of 79 tested homes above 200 Bq/m³, against 12.1% for the Winnipeg health region. In the 2024 survey Brandon was at 36.1% and Winnipeg at 37.4%, but both are unweighted volunteer samples and Brandon's is small. The fair conclusion is that radon is common in both cities and you can't predict a single house from a city average.

### Does the Brandon library lend radon detectors?

The Western Manitoba Regional Library is a partner in the Manitoba Lung Association's lending program, with Brandon among its listed locations. We couldn't find the loan length or season, so check with the library before you go. A borrowed monitor is only a screening tool, and a long-term test is what Health Canada recommends for decisions.

### How much does radon mitigation cost in Brandon?

We couldn't find a published Brandon price. ### Is there a law in Manitoba requiring me to test for radon?

### Can I get financial help to fix radon in Brandon?

Possibly. The Manitoba Hydro Home Energy Efficiency Loan covers radon mitigation up to $5,000 with a C-NRPP certified contractor, and Lungs Matter offers up to $1,500 for eligible households. Both have conditions, so read the current terms before you book any work.
