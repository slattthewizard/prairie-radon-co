Winnipeg installers advertise radon systems from about $1,500 to $3,000. If your basement test came back high and that range made you wince, you should know that Manitoba has exactly one radon mitigation grant and one loan, and the order you do things in decides whether you can use either of them.

The grant is Lungs Matter, run in this province by the Manitoba Lung Association. It pays up to $1,500 toward a system. The loan is Manitoba Hydro's Home Energy Efficiency Loan, which covers radon mitigation up to $5,000 and goes on your energy bill.

Hardly anyone uses them. The Made-in-Manitoba Radon Action Plan says only 18 Manitoba households used Lungs Matter funds in 2024, and that Hydro financed an average of about seven mitigation systems a year over the last decade. If you qualify, you are not fighting a crowd, though the Canadian Lung Association does warn that funding is limited.

## Lungs Matter: the one radon mitigation grant in Manitoba

The Manitoba Lung Association says it plainly: "Lungs Matter is the only radon mitigation grant program offered in Manitoba." It pays up to $1,500 toward mitigation done by a radon mitigation professional.

Two groups qualify in Manitoba. The first is households with low to moderate income, with priority going to low-income households. The second is anyone diagnosed with lung cancer, regardless of income.

The national version, run by the Canadian Lung Association, spells out the paperwork. You need a CRA Notice of Assessment showing household income below the provincial median. You need a long-term result of at least 90 days from a C-NRPP certified test kit, reading above 200 Bq/m3. And you need a quote from a C-NRPP certified mitigation professional.

A word on that unit. Bq/m3 means becquerels per cubic metre, a measure of how much radioactive decay is happening in a given volume of air. Health Canada's guideline is 200 Bq/m3, about 5.4 pCi/L if you're comparing with US numbers. It is a guideline, not a law: nothing in Manitoba requires you to test or fix anything. Above 200, Health Canada recommends corrective action within a year.

The Manitoba Lung Association assesses applications monthly. Full details are on its [Lungs Matter page](https://www.mb.lung.ca/lungs_matter).

## Manitoba Hydro's loan: radon mitigation financing without an income test

The Home Energy Efficiency Loan is the option for everyone who earns too much for Lungs Matter. Manitoba Hydro lends up to $5,000 for radon mitigation over 5 years at 6.2% a year, on approved credit. Payments go on your energy bill. There's no down payment and no income threshold.

The one hard rule: the mitigation has to be done by a C-NRPP certified contractor. A handyman's system, or one you put in yourself, won't qualify. The loan also counts toward Hydro's limit of $12,500 per residence, so if you've already financed a furnace or insulation through the same program, check how much room is left.

A loan is still debt. At 6.2% you'll pay more than the sticker price over five years. Your contractor or Hydro can show you the monthly figure for your actual quote, and it's worth seeing it in writing before you sign.

## The order that keeps you eligible for Manitoba radon funding

Most people who miss out on help do it by getting the steps in the wrong order. Here's the sequence that keeps both doors open.

1. **Test with a long-term kit.** At least 90 days, ideally in the heating season, in the lowest level you actually live in. Health Canada's minimum is 91 days; anything shorter should be repeated. Our [long-term vs short-term test guide](/blog/long-term-vs-short-term-radon-test/) explains why a quick reading isn't enough.
2. **Check that everyone is C-NRPP certified.** Measurement and mitigation are separate certifications. You can confirm a professional in C-NRPP's online directory. Our [explainer on C-NRPP certification](/blog/what-is-c-nrpp-certification/) walks through it.
3. **Get the quote in writing.** Both programs want to see one.
4. **Apply before any work starts.** The Canadian Lung Association says Lungs Matter isn't available for mitigations already completed.
5. **Book the install once the money is sorted.**
6. **Retest afterwards.** Health Canada says to test again after mitigation. A system that isn't checked is a guess.

Why bother with all this? Health Canada calls radon the number one cause of lung cancer in non-smokers and estimates it's behind more than 3,000 lung cancer deaths in Canada each year ([Health Canada, Radon: What you need to know](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/environmental-workplace-health/reports-publications/radon-what-you-need-to-know.html)). That's the reason to test. It isn't a reason to panic, and it isn't a reason to skip the paperwork that could save you $1,500.

A few things will not help. Opening windows or running a basement fan is not a fix, and there's no grant for either. Neither is a quote from someone who can't show a C-NRPP certification, because neither program will pay for their work.

## In Regina or Saskatoon? What Saskatchewan homeowners can use

Most of this article is about Manitoba, but plenty of readers are across the border, so here's the Saskatchewan picture. It's thinner.

The national Lungs Matter program from the Canadian Lung Association is open to Saskatchewan residents on the same terms: household income below the provincial median, a 90-day C-NRPP test above 200 Bq/m3, a C-NRPP quote, and no work already done. Lung Saskatchewan has offered its own reimbursement in the past; that program is paused for now, so check its site for news before you plan around it.

The Saskatchewan Home Renovation Tax Credit is 10.5% on up to $4,000 of eligible expenses, which works out to savings of up to $420 (seniors up to $525 on $5,000). Expenses must be from 1 October 2024 onward, and only the amount above a $1,000 base counts. The government's list doesn't name radon, but Lung Saskatchewan says radon mitigation counts as an eligible expense; confirm with CRA before you rely on it. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is there a radon mitigation grant in Manitoba for every homeowner?

No. Lungs Matter is for low- to moderate-income households, or anyone diagnosed with lung cancer, and it pays up to $1,500. If you earn more than that, the Manitoba Hydro Home Energy Efficiency Loan has no income test and lends up to $5,000 for radon mitigation.

### Can I apply with a result from a library radon monitor?

Not for Lungs Matter. The Manitoba Lung Association says homeowner-level digital monitors don't qualify, and the grant needs a long-term 90-day C-NRPP approved test kit. A library monitor is still useful for deciding whether to test properly.

### Can I use Lungs Matter and the Manitoba Hydro loan together?

We haven't found a rule either way on either program's pages. Ask the Manitoba Lung Association and Manitoba Hydro directly before you sign a contract. Keep in mind the grant can't be used for work that's already been done, so apply before the install.

### Is there a federal grant or tax credit for radon mitigation?

There is currently no federal radon tax credit that we could find. In Canada, the help that exists runs through lung associations, Manitoba Hydro and, in Saskatchewan, the provincial Home Renovation Tax Credit. Check each program's own page, because terms and funding change.
